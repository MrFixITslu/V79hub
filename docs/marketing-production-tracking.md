# Production publications in Marketing records

A campaign approval authorizes its exact destination and future schedule; it is not an upload receipt. A successful provider response is first retained in the signed production ledger. Only confirmed `succeeded` deliveries can project into the ordinary `posts` and `post_deliveries` tables, with status `PUBLISHED`, the exact account, original content/visibility/schedule, provider ID and receipt time. Each delivery has one deterministic post ID and a `production_post_links` provenance row. Metrics remain null until real analytics exist.

The provenance table is an additive startup migration. It retains source package/delivery digests and durable asset names/hashes without making private drafts public. Existing posts are never replaced on an identifier conflict. Author and workspace mapping must still match. The normal content calendar and delivery API include these records, identify their production origin, and display a full canonical YouTube watch URL from a valid confirmed video ID. Marketing polls both posts and deliveries, so an open calendar receives newly tracked publications.

Receipt persistence precedes projection. A projection transaction includes both Marketing records and the signed tracking acknowledgement. If it fails, the signed successful receipt remains intact. Subsequent worker cycles retry only this local projection, including receipts saved before the upgrade. Running and uncertain uploads are never treated as confirmed receipts, and repairing a Marketing record never calls a provider. Existing projections are checked and conflicts require reconciliation rather than overwrite.

Production posts are excluded from the general publisher by both their reserved ID namespace and their provenance link. The ordinary retry route refuses them. Recording a provider success cannot therefore add a second publishing path or turn an uncertain outcome into an automatic retry. Approval, signature, MFA, destination and entitlement checks remain in the production workflow.

The Hub derives its package heading from delivery states instead of leaving a completed or approved package labeled `awaiting_owner`. Individual deliveries distinguish approval waiting for its scheduled time, upload/publishing in progress, a confirmed receipt, and uncertain outcomes. Mixed destinations keep outstanding approvals visible. A confirmed YouTube upload displays its full link, original visibility, receipt time and Marketing tracking acknowledgement. Private visibility and provider processing still govern playback. The failed-attempt cleanup and retained canceled history are included in the same Hub update.

Validation: the full Marketing suite passed (84 tests at the first complete run), followed by an additional regression proving a local receipt-read failure cannot downgrade a successful upload. The network-isolated Vitest configuration also passed the selected production/dispatch suites; seven tracking tests cover exact content, unique records, tenant isolation, tampering, collision safety, atomic projection failure/recovery and no provider replay. The general publisher regression excludes both reserved and linked production IDs even if their post status is changed. Hub's 22 targeted component/approval/G2 tests pass. TypeScript and both production builds pass. Real browser acceptance and copied live-state restore checks are required after the candidate images are built; these local tests make no claim of live deployment or real provider upload success.
